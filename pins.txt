ESP32-S3 pin usage — new custom PCB
Updated 2026-10-15 from firmware/Motor_Drive_With_IDF/main/main.c (the pre-PCB pinout
from 2026-08-12 is gone; see git history if you need it).

=====================================================================
ROBOT WIRING (new PCB — TB6612FNG + encoders + GY-85 + battery sense)
=====================================================================
GPIO 4   PWMA    motor A PWM
GPIO 6   AIN1    motor A direction 1
GPIO 5   AIN2    motor A direction 2
GPIO 7   STBY    TB6612 standby (HIGH = driver enabled)
GPIO 17  PWMB    motor B PWM
GPIO 15  BIN1    motor B direction 1
GPIO 16  BIN2    motor B direction 2

Encoders (quadrature, 4x decode = 2 PCNT channels per motor, 1980 ticks per wheel turn):
GPIO 11  enAA    motor A encoder  (defined as "phase A" but really the other phase)
GPIO 10  enAB    motor A encoder
GPIO 13  enBA    motor B encoder
GPIO 12  enBB    motor B encoder
  The two pins of each motor are swapped on purpose in the #defines so that
  driving forward counts POSITIVE. Swap them back and the sign flips.

GPIO 9   sda     I2C SDA (GY-85)
GPIO 8   scl     I2C SCL (GY-85)
GPIO 14  vsense  battery voltage, ADC2 channel 3, divider 100k (top) / 22k (bottom)
                 battery V = pin V x 5.545 (plus a measured gain of ~1.09 on this board)
GPIO 48  RGB LED on the dev board (WS2812-type). Not driven by the firmware yet, so it
         floats and flickers; set it as an output and drive it low to keep it quiet.

History kept from the old inventory:
- Old encoder pins GPIO 3 and 46 were ESP32-S3 strapping pins (sampled at reset). The new
  PCB moved the encoders to GPIO 10-13, so that open question no longer applies.
- RESOLVED 2026-08-13: GPIO 18 was wired as a fake "GND" driven LOW by firmware. That left the
  TB6612 logic ground floating until the ESP32 booted, so the motor only ran if USB power came
  up before the battery. The wire went to a real GND pin and the GND define was removed.
- Not done: a formal strapping-pin / PSRAM-range / power-topology audit (Tutorial 2's job).
  The new pin map was only checked on the bench (2026-09-05), not audited.

Serial: the native USB port (303a) is output-only for the console; keyboard input in
`idf.py monitor` needs the other USB port (WCH 1a86, console on UART0).

=====================================================================
STANDALONE LEARNING EXAMPLES (separate projects, not built/wired together)
These use the OLD pin choices on a bare dev board. On the PCB, GPIO 9 is the I2C SDA.
=====================================================================
LED_Blink/main/main.c
  GPIO 9   LED_GPIO      output, blinking LED

LED_Blink_Use_GPIO_Config/main/main.c
  GPIO 9   LED_GPIO      output, LED
  GPIO 46  switch_GPIO   input, pull-up, polled switch

pwm_project/main/main.c
  GPIO 9   LED_GPIO      output, PWM via LEDC channel 0
  GPIO 46  button_gpio   defined, not wired into this file's logic

Interupt_example/main/main.c
  GPIO 46  LED_GPIO      output, blinking LED
  GPIO 9   BUTTON_GPIO   input, pull-up, rising-edge ISR + debounce

Analog_Input/main/main.c
  ADC_CHANNEL_8 (ADC1)   -> GPIO 9 on ESP32-S3 (per Espressif's ADC1
                            channel table — double-check against the
                            datasheet, don't take my word for it)

robot_2wd/src/main.c (Tutorial 1 project)
  no GPIO used — log-only over the USB serial console

Note: GPIO 9 and GPIO 46 are reused with swapped roles between
LED_Blink_Use_GPIO_Config and Interupt_example. That's harmless since
these are separate, never-simultaneous builds.
